One-tap help from someone you trust

Help when they need it. The know-how for next time.

AssistMe connects an older adult with a family member or friend they trust. They tap one button and your phone rings. You see their screen live, talk them through it and draw on it to point the way. When you hang up, they keep a private guide that Clicky replays on their own phone, offline.

02How it works

Grandma does one thing. You do the rest.

Most remote-support tools are built for IT desks, with accounts, session codes and settings to explain. AssistMe is built for the person on the other end of the phone call.

  1. Once

    Pair with a 6-digit code

    She shows a code, you type it, she approves. No email, no password. Codes expire after 15 minutes.

  2. Her side

    One tap to call

    She taps your name and your phone rings. She can say what's wrong, or not.

  3. Her side

    She shares, and stays in charge

    Android asks before anything is shared. A big STOP button stays on her screen, and only she can press it.

  4. Your side

    See it, say it, draw it

    You see her screen live and circle the button to press. With her permission, you can tap for her.

  5. After the call

    Cover anything private

    You review each captured step and block out passwords or messages. Covered pixels are gone for good.

  6. After the call

    The guide writes itself

    Whisper turns the call into text and Gemma drafts the steps, both on your phone. You check them and send.

  7. Next time

    Clicky shows the way

    She opens the guide and taps Use Clicky. It reads each step aloud and points at the button to press. No internet needed.

03Features

Everything a phone call can't do.

Live screen and voice

Screen sharing at 30 fps with two-way audio, phone to phone. A relay takes over when networks get in the way.

WebRTC · up to 2.5 Mbps · STUN/TURN

Draw over any app

Your strokes appear on top of whatever she has open, even above the notification shade.

~32 ms preview batches

Control, only with consent

Taps, swipes and long presses work only after she approves a clear warning. Password, banking and permission screens are detected.

Android Accessibility Service

Guides written on the phone

Whisper transcribes the call, only if she agrees, and Gemma drafts the steps. Neither ever leaves the helper's phone.

Whisper tiny + Gemma 4 E2B · LiteRT

Clicky, the offline walkthrough

A floating card speaks each step and a pointer flies to the button. It handles press-and-hold and swipes, and Back undoes a step.

Matches buttons by label · never taps

Guides that stay private

Sent over the call itself, or later as ciphertext the server can't read. On the phone they're encrypted with a hardware-backed key.

AES-256-GCM · Android Keystore

Calls that don't drop

Calls survive switching from Wi-Fi to mobile data, dropped sockets and app restarts.

Redis-backed signalling

Nothing to remember

No email, password or meeting code. The phone is the identity, with a one-time recovery code for a new phone.

Argon2id-hashed recovery

Missed calls aren't lost

Calls ring over a persistent connection, and a call you missed is waiting when you open the app. Add a Firebase project to ring phones whose app is closed.

WebSocket · optional Firebase push
04Privacy

The server connects you. It never sees the call.

Screen, voice, drawings and guides travel straight between the two phones. The backend only pairs devices, rings phones and passes connection details.

Never reaches the server

  • Screenshots. They stay on the helper's phone until reviewed, then go to her phone over the call.
  • Call audio. Recorded only if she agrees, at most 20 minutes, deleted right after transcription.
  • Guide text. If she has already hung up, it is uploaded as ciphertext the server can't read.
  • Anything you covered. Redacted pixels are replaced with solid blocks before the AI or anyone else sees them.

06Limits

What to know before you install it.

Android only

Both phones need Android 11 or newer. The iOS code paths exist but aren't maintained.

The AI needs a capable phone

On-device guides need a 64-bit ARM phone with several GB of RAM and a one-time download of about 2.75 GB. The app checks before downloading anything.

Clicky reads labels

Clicky finds buttons by their on-screen names. Icons without a label, or screens that look very different from the guide, still need a tap on Next.

Bring your own server

The release points at a demo server that's only online during demos. Run the backend yourself with Docker and add your own Firebase project to ring phones whose app is closed.

07Install

Two phones and ten minutes.

Install the app on both phones

Download the APK from the latest release and open it on each phone. Pick a name, then turn on the AssistMe accessibility service.

Latest release

Run the backend

Copy the example settings, replace every secret, and start the stack.

cp deploy/.env.example deploy/.env
cd deploy
docker compose -f docker-compose.yml \
  -f docker-compose.local.yml up -d --build
docker compose run --rm migrate

Point the phones at it

On each phone open Settings → Custom Backend Server and enter your server's HTTPS address. For guides, download the AI models on the helper's phone.
